The [Learn section](/learn/) is finished. Every topic across all seven sections is written, published, and free to read, no email required, no paywall halfway through. It took from the middle of July to the end of August to get here, and it's the single largest thing on this site.

---

## What's in it

Sixty-one lessons, organised into seven sections that build on each other in order.

**Foundations** covers where markets came from and how they work, the basic vocabulary every trader needs before anything else makes sense: leverage, spreads, sessions, the difference between a broker and a platform. **Reading Charts** moves on to candlesticks, chart types, support and resistance, trendlines, and what indicators are doing under the hood. **Market Structure** goes deeper: trends, ranges, supply and demand, fair value gaps, order blocks, Elliott Waves, and reading more than one timeframe at once.

**Price Action** is about trading what the chart is doing directly instead of waiting for an indicator to confirm it, breakouts, pullbacks, failed setups, and how to find confluence between them. **Risk Management** is the section most traders skip and shouldn't: position sizing, where a stop belongs, risk-to-reward, daily loss limits, and the specific rules that come with a funded prop firm account. **Psychology** covers the part that has nothing to do with charts at all: fear, greed, discipline, revenge trading, burnout, and building a routine that survives contact with a losing week. **Execution** ties the whole thing together: writing a real trading plan, preparing for a session, getting into and managing a trade properly, and knowing when to stop.

Most topics have a diagram where a picture earns its place, and the trading plan topic comes with a **free downloadable template**, twelve sections, ready to fill in with your own numbers.

## Why this exists

Free trading education is everywhere and most of it is a mess: a YouTube video here, a forum thread from 2011 there, a paid course promising a "proven system" that's really just someone else's specific setup dressed up as a universal one. Very little of it is organised as an actual curriculum, something you could start on day one and work through in order, each topic building on the last instead of assuming you already know half of what came before it.

That's the gap this fills. No promises of guaranteed profits, no signals, no "copy my trades." Just how markets, charts, and risk work, explained plainly, in the order a real beginner would need to learn them, sitting next to the same honest, no-hype approach the rest of this site tries to take.

---

## Who it's for

Two kinds of readers, really. If you're starting from nothing, **Foundations** is the place to begin, and the sections after it are ordered deliberately: work through them in sequence and each one assumes only what the last one taught you.

If you already trade and just have a gap, each of the 61 topics stands on its own. Jump straight to Position Sizing, Stop Loss Placement, or Managing the Trade without reading the 40 lessons before it, nothing in those specific pages assumes you've done the rest of the curriculum first.

## Where to start

If you're new to this: [Foundations](/learn/foundations/). If you already know the basics and want to check for gaps: the [full Learn index](/learn/) lists all seven sections and every topic in each one. And if you're building a trading plan for the first time, the [Trading Plan](/learn/execution/trading-plan/) lesson and its downloadable template are a reasonable place to start regardless of experience.

This isn't the end of it, some of the earlier sections were written before the later ones and could use another pass for depth over time, but the curriculum itself, start to finish, is done.
