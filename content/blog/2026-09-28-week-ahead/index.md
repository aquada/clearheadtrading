It was a choppier week than the closing numbers suggest. Bond yields surged again in the middle of the week, unsettling all three indices for a day or two, before oil prices eased and an AI-driven rally in chip and tech names pulled everything back up by Friday. NASD did more than recover: Meta, AMD, and Intel led it to a fresh all-time high close mid-week, and it finished Friday just a fraction of a percent below that record. Dow, meanwhile, snapped a three-week losing streak.

## Last week

DAX added 0.4% to close at 25,408.64, Dow gained 0.3% to 51,828.62 on a strong 478-point Friday, and NASD jumped 2.1% to 27,068.72. NASD's gain came almost entirely from a two-day burst - a fresh closing record of 27,244.28 and an intraday high of 27,288.79, both set midweek - as investors piled back into the same AI and chip names that have driven most of this year's tech gains.

There's little Germany-specific news due this week, so the index is likely to keep taking its cue from Wall Street's own reaction to Thursday and Friday's US data. A close below 25,120 would point further down; clearing 25,740 would put the index back on track toward its own highs.

### Dow

<!-- Weekly pivot from last week's H/L/C (52,319.01 / 51,124.02 / 51,828.62). -->
Dow has support around 51,195 and resistance around 52,390, with its record high of 54,349 still standing well above both. Friday's rebound came from the same yields-easing, oil-easing combination lifting everything else, so this week's jobs data matters more here than almost anywhere else - rate-sensitive sectors like banks and industrials react fastest to anything that changes the outlook for borrowing costs.

### NASD

<!-- Weekly pivot from last week's H/L/C (27,288.79 / 26,706.14 / 27,068.72). -->
NASD has support around 26,754 and resistance around 27,336 - just above its own record close of 27,244.28 and the week's intraday high of 27,288.79. This is about as close to a real breakout as the index has been all year: clearing that would open new territory outright, while a slip back below 26,754 would suggest last week's rally ran ahead of itself. Given how much of this move rests on a handful of AI and chip names, a soft earnings surprise from any of them could hit NASD harder than the other two.

## Elsewhere this week

Oil eased to around $102.75 a barrel on Friday on reports of progress toward defusing the Strait of Hormuz standoff, only for that progress to unravel again first thing this week: Brent jumped back above $105 after the US rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the strait, pulling equity futures lower with it on Monday morning. Gold held around $4,341 an ounce, still recovering from the low it hit two weeks ago. Bitcoin traded near $84,000, extending its own recovery. EURUSD slipped toward the 1.1375 area as the dollar stayed firm.

## The calendar

Congress passed a stopgap funding bill through 11 December, removing the government-shutdown risk that had been hanging over the last day of the month - one less thing for markets to price in going into an unusually busy data week. **Monday** and **Tuesday** are quiet by comparison. **Wednesday** carries the usual mid-week data flow but nothing central. **Thursday** brings August's core PCE reading, the Fed's own preferred inflation gauge, last seen at 3.3% year-on-year and still well above its 2% target. **Friday** is the real event: September's jobs report, expected to show a sharp slowdown from August's 162,000 to something closer to 50,000 by some estimates. A number that soft, so soon after the Fed's first rate rise in three years, would raise real questions about whether the central bank can keep raising rates without a labour market that's already cooling faster than it looks.
