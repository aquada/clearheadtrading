Wednesday's inflation report was the week's turning point. September's consumer prices rose 3.7% year on year, hotter than the 3.6% forecast, and that was enough to revive worries about another Fed rate rise just as investors were already nervous about how much money has been poured into AI infrastructure and how exposed US chipmakers are to Chinese competition. Chip stocks bore the brunt of it, and NASD never really recovered even as Thursday brought a partial rebound. DAX and Dow told a different story: both held up far better, and DAX actually finished the week higher, carried by defence and auto stocks rather than anything to do with inflation at all.

## Last week

NASD fell 4.5% to close at 26,140.03, its worst week since June, as Micron dropped 5.6% and Intel lost 5.8% on Friday alone. Dow added 0.7% to 52,002.41, with energy stocks helping cushion the broader tech-led weakness. DAX gained 2.1% to 25,622.42, helped by Rheinmetall's 6.1% jump as European leaders met in Brussels to reaffirm support for Ukraine, plus solid gains for BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen on hopes of more flexible EU emissions rules.

## The week ahead

### DAX

DAX has [support](/learn/reading-charts/support-and-resistance/) around 25,273 and resistance around 25,833. Last week's strength came from company-specific stories, defence and autos, rather than anything macro, so the index looks better placed to shrug off a rough week on Wall Street than it would in a typical risk-off move. A close below 25,273 would suggest the global mood is starting to weigh on German stocks after all; clearing 25,833 would put the index back within range of its record highs.

### Dow

<!-- Weekly pivots: H 52,582.32 / L 51,365.42 / settled close 52,002.41: P 51,983, S1 51,384, R1 52,601. -->
Dow has support around 51,384 and resistance around 52,601, with its record high of 54,349 still standing above both. Dow has now held up through two straight weeks of tech wobbles, largely because it carries so much less exposure to the AI and chip names driving the selling. Netflix and Johnson & Johnson both report Tuesday, and while neither moves the index much on its own, a broadly strong start to earnings season would help keep that resilience intact.

### NASD

<!-- Weekly pivots: H 27,376.66 / L 26,023.10 / settled close 26,140.03: P 26,513, S1 25,650, R1 27,003. -->
NASD has support around 25,650 and resistance around 27,003, with its own record close of 27,244.28 now more than 4% above where it finished the week. Tesla reports Tuesday, and given how much of this week's selling was about AI spending and chip exposure specifically, its results and especially its tone on AI-related capital spending are likely to matter more than usual. A close below 25,650 would point to the selling having further to run; climbing back above 27,003 would be the clearest sign the AI-bubble worries are starting to fade rather than build.

## Elsewhere this week

Oil pushed higher again, with Brent near $110 a barrel and WTI around $101, as the Strait of Hormuz standoff showed no sign of easing. Gold held steady near $4,181 an ounce. Bitcoin slipped to around $81,600, giving back some of the previous week's gain as the same risk-off mood that hit chip stocks spread into crypto. EURUSD eased to about 1.1240 as the hot inflation print firmed up the dollar.

## The calendar

This week leans heavily on earnings rather than data. **Monday** sees GE Aerospace report, among the first wave of industrial names this earnings season. **Tuesday** is the busiest day, with Netflix, Johnson & Johnson and Tesla all due to report. **Wednesday** and **Thursday** carry a steady stream of further earnings without a single standout name. **Friday** brings the flash reading of October's US manufacturing and services PMIs, the first broad read on how businesses are responding to everything that's happened this month. With the Fed's next meeting not due until 27-28 October, this week's earnings are likely to matter more for sentiment than anything on the economic calendar.
