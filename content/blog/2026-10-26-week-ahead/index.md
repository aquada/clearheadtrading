Last week's main talking point carried straight through from the one before it: AI spending and how much of it is really paying off. Meta's results on Wednesday only added to the unease, with the company beating on headline profit but raising its 2026 capital spending guidance to as much as $145 billion, more than enough to keep chip and AI-adjacent names under pressure. DAX gave back a good part of its own gains from the week before, Dow kept holding up better than its tech-heavy peers, and NASD extended the rough patch it's been in for weeks now. This week moves the spotlight from one week's earnings call to the main event: the Fed's rate decision on Wednesday, with Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Apple all due to report either side of it.

## Last week

DAX fell 1.7% to close at 25,181.65, giving back a chunk of the previous week's gains as some of the enthusiasm around Rheinmetall and the carmakers cooled and investors took profits after the index's best week in a while. Dow added a modest 0.4% to 52,215.16, continuing to hold up better than its more tech-heavy peers thanks to its lighter weighting in chip and AI-adjacent names. NASD (the Nasdaq-100) slipped a further 0.8% to 28,957.53, extending its rough patch as Meta's Wednesday earnings, despite the headline beat, renewed worries about AI spending after the company lifted its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to as much as $145 billion.

NASD (the Nasdaq-100) has support around 28,826, Friday's own low, with the base it broke through a week ago, around 29,930, still standing overhead as the nearest resistance, and its record high of 31,405 well above that. Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Apple all report this week, and after Meta's capital spending guidance rattled sentiment, their own AI-spending numbers are likely to matter more than the usual revenue and profit lines. A close below 28,826 would suggest the AI-spending worries still have room to run; reclaiming 29,930 would be the clearest sign they're starting to fade.

## Elsewhere this week

Oil eased back a touch, with Brent near $109 a barrel and WTI around $100, as the risk-off mood that hit chip stocks took some heat out of the demand outlook too. Gold firmed to around $4,205 an ounce on the same safe-haven bid. Bitcoin slipped further to about $80,200, tracking the weakness in risk assets generally. EURUSD held close to 1.1255, broadly steady as traders waited for the Fed before committing to a direction.

## The calendar

This week is dominated by the Fed and a wave of Big Tech results. **Monday** is quiet, with little scheduled ahead of the main events. **Tuesday** sees the Fed's two-day meeting get under way, alongside earnings from Microsoft and Alphabet after the close. **Wednesday** brings the Fed's rate decision and press conference in the afternoon, the clear highlight of the week. **Thursday** continues the earnings wave with Amazon and Apple reporting after the close. **Friday** rounds things off with the first estimate of US third-quarter GDP, a useful read on how the economy was holding up before this week's events landed.
