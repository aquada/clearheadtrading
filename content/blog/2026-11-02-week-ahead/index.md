The Fed held its policy rate steady on Wednesday, as expected, and the decision itself did little to move markets either way. What did move markets was Thursday's earnings from Amazon: a revenue miss sent the stock sharply lower after hours and reignited the AI-spending worries that have been simmering for weeks, triggering a broad tech selloff on Friday. DAX, largely insulated from a story that's really about US mega-cap spending plans, pushed to its highest level in weeks. NASD bore the brunt of it, and Dow, which had held up well for most of the week, got caught up in the Friday weakness too.

## Last week

DAX gained 2.5% to close at 25,818.21, touching a fresh multi-week high of 26,039.68 on Wednesday as the Fed's steady-as-she-goes decision helped calm nerves, before easing back slightly on Friday as the US tech rout spread some caution. Dow slipped 0.9% to 51,770.31, holding close to flat through midweek before Friday's broader risk-off mood, spilling over from the Nasdaq selloff, dragged it lower into the close. NASD (the Nasdaq-100) fell 2.0% to 28,384.85, its lowest close in months, as Amazon's revenue miss and continuing questions about AI capital spending triggered a sharp slide across chip and mega-cap tech names on Friday.

NASD (the Nasdaq-100) has support around 28,095, Friday's own low after a sharp break with no shelf underneath it, and resistance around 29,930, the same level it failed to clear twice this week before Friday's selloff. This is the index with the most riding on how the AI-spending story develops from here, and Friday's jobs report matters for it too, since a hot number would add rate worries on top of the capex concerns already driving the selling. A close below 28,095 would suggest the selling has further to go; reclaiming 29,930 would be the clearest sign confidence is returning.

## Elsewhere this week

Oil eased further, with Brent near $108 a barrel and WTI around $99, as the risk-off mood took a little more demand optimism out of the price. Gold pushed higher to around $4,250 an ounce on safe-haven buying. Bitcoin fell further to about $77,500, tracking the broader pullback in risk assets. EURUSD held near 1.1230, with traders reluctant to take a strong view ahead of Friday's jobs report.

## The calendar

This week's calendar builds toward Friday. **Monday** brings October's ISM manufacturing PMI, the first broad read on US factory activity since the Fed's decision. **Tuesday** sees September's JOLTS job openings data. **Wednesday** brings October's ISM services PMI, a sector that's held up better than manufacturing through most of the year. **Thursday** is comparatively quiet. **Friday** is the week's main event: October's US jobs report, due at 08:30 ET, which will shape how markets read the odds of a December rate move after a Fed decision that left the question open.
