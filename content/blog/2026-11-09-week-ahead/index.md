Tuesday's US midterm elections set off one of the choppier weeks in months. Markets rallied hard on Wednesday as Democrats took both chambers of Congress, with traders reading the result as political gridlock that limits the chance of sweeping new legislation. That relief didn't last: Thursday brought a sharp reversal as investors started questioning whether a shift in Washington could slow the build-out of AI data centres, triggering a broad selloff that hit chip and AI-linked names hardest and pushed NASD into correction territory. Friday brought a partial rebound, though a stronger-than-expected October jobs report added a fresh complication by pushing bets on a December rate hike back up.

## Last week

DAX closed the week almost exactly where it started, at 25,820.36, after a choppier path than that flat number suggests: it touched a fresh record high of 26,692.90 on Thursday before the AI selloff spread from US markets and pulled it back down. Dow fell 0.9% to 51,285.73, having been up as much as 3% on Wednesday's midterm relief rally before Thursday's 1,833-point plunge wiped most of that out. NASD (the Nasdaq-100) bore the brunt of it, tumbling 4.9% to 26,989.99 and briefly dropping into correction territory (commonly defined as a 10% fall from a recent high) on Thursday, before Friday's bounce clawed back some of the week's losses.

## Elsewhere this week

Oil fell sharply, with Brent down to around $98 a barrel and WTI near $91, as the midterm result eased some of the geopolitical risk premium baked into the price. Gold eased back to around $4,070 an ounce, giving back part of its post-election gain as Friday's strong jobs report lifted the dollar. Bitcoin dropped further to around $76,000, tracking the broader pullback in risk assets through Thursday's selloff. EURUSD slipped to around 1.114 as the stronger jobs data and rising hike odds supported the dollar.
