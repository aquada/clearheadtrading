The sharp AI-driven selloff from the week before gave way to an equally sharp rebound. All three main indices posted strong weekly gains, with DAX touching a fresh all-time high on Thursday as the worst of the correction fears eased. The recovery wasn't in a straight line, though: Tuesday's October inflation report came in hotter than expected, and by Thursday and Friday, caution ahead of Nvidia's earnings and the hot CPI print had chipped away at some of the week's gains, particularly on NASD.

## Last week

DAX gained 3.6% to close at 26,749.95, having set a fresh all-time intraday high of 27,001.67 on Thursday (closing record 26,771.88) before easing back slightly on Friday. Dow rose 2.1% to 52,347.55, its best week in months as the relief from the prior week's selloff carried through most of the five days. NASD (the Nasdaq-100) gained 3.5% to 27,943.74, with most of that gain coming early in the week; Tuesday's hotter-than-expected October inflation data and growing caution ahead of Nvidia's earnings this week took back some of the ground on Thursday and Friday.

## The week ahead

A close below 26,680 would suggest the index needs to digest its recent gains a while longer; clearing 27,000 would open genuinely uncharted territory.

### Dow

Dow has support around 52,020, Friday's low, with resistance around 52,750, Thursday's high and the strongest level the index reached this week; its record high of 54,796 sits well above that. Tuesday's US retail sales report is the one to watch, since a strong number would add to the case for a bigger Fed move in December on top of this week's already-hot inflation print. A close below 52,020 would point to this week's rebound losing steam; reclaiming 52,750 would put the index back on track toward its record.

### NASD

NASD (the Nasdaq-100) has support around 27,610, Friday's low, with a deeper floor near 27,230 if that doesn't hold, and resistance around 28,590, Wednesday's high, where the rally stalled before hot inflation data and Nvidia-earnings nerves took over. Nvidia reports after Tuesday's close, and with the stock already down sharply from its summer peak on AI-spending worries, this is the single biggest event of the week for this index — a strong report could go a long way toward settling the AI-spending debate that's driven most of the last fortnight's swings, while a disappointment could easily reopen last week's selloff. A close below 27,610 would suggest the correction fears haven't fully passed; reclaiming 28,590 would be the clearest sign the rebound has further to run.

## Elsewhere this week

Oil held fairly steady, with Brent around $98 a barrel and WTI near $91. Gold eased slightly to around $4,070 an ounce as the stronger inflation data and firmer rate expectations took some shine off safe havens. Bitcoin held broadly steady in the mid-$70,000s after the prior week's selloff. EURUSD stayed close to 1.114, with traders reluctant to take a strong view ahead of this week's Fed commentary and data.
