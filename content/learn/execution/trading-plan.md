A trading plan turns every rule covered elsewhere in this course, entry criteria, position size, when to stop, into a single document written before a trade goes live, so none of it has to be decided under pressure.

---

## What belongs in a working plan

A working plan is specific enough that two different days produce the same decision given the same chart: the exact conditions that count as a valid entry, the risk per trade and how position size gets calculated from it, where the stop and target come from, which instruments and timeframes it applies to, and the session hours it's meant to be traded in. It should also say when it doesn't apply, around major scheduled news, in illiquid overnight hours, on a broken platform connection. A plan silent on its own limits gets stretched to cover situations it was never built for.

---

## A document, not a memory

Something "known in your head" and something written down behave very differently under pressure. A vague intention is easy to bend in the moment it's tested, because there's nothing concrete to check it against. A written rule, entry criteria worded specifically enough to say yes or no, is much harder to quietly reinterpret mid-trade. Writing the plan down doesn't make a trader more disciplined by itself, but it removes one of the easiest ways discipline slips: not remembering, in the moment, what was decided in advance.

---

## It needs testing before it needs following

A plan on paper is a hypothesis, not a proven edge, until it's been checked against real trades or historical data. Committing hard to an untested plan simply because it's written down and looks professional mistakes structure for validity, two different things. A plan earns the confidence to follow it strictly through a track record, not through how organised it looks on the page.

---

## Key takeaways

- A working plan is specific enough that the same setup produces the same decision every time, not just a general intention
- It should also state when it doesn't apply, around news, illiquid hours, or platform issues
- Writing it down removes one of the easiest ways discipline slips: forgetting, under pressure, what was already decided
- A plan is a hypothesis until tested against real results, not something to follow strictly just because it's written down
