A strategy decides what to trade, a routine decides how the session around it runs, and a repeatable routine matters as much as the strategy itself, because most of the mistakes covered elsewhere in this section happen in the gaps a routine is supposed to fill.

---

## Before the session

Preparation before the market opens does two things at once: it puts the day's real catalysts, scheduled data, key levels, anything that changes the plan, in view before they can surprise a live position, and it gives the trader a calm moment to commit to the day's rules before pressure has a chance to bend them. A quick routine, checking the calendar, marking the levels that matter, reviewing the plan's risk limits for the day, takes a few minutes and closes off a lot of the drift that shows up later. [Session Preparation](/learn/execution/session-preparation/) and [Pre-Session Checklist](/learn/execution/pre-session-checklist/) cover this stage in full.

---

## During the session

A session with a defined start and end time behaves differently to one that just continues until the trader happens to stop, because an open-ended session removes the one honest signal that it might be time to walk away. Fixed hours don't guarantee good decisions on their own, but they remove the excuse to keep going past the point where focus and judgement have already started to slip, which several other topics in this section cover from different angles.

---

## After the session

The review that happens after the session closes matters more than most traders give it credit for, and it's not the same thing as checking the day's P&L. A proper review looks at whether each trade matched the plan's criteria regardless of whether it won or lost, because a losing trade taken correctly and a winning trade taken outside the rules deserve the opposite reaction to the one most traders give them instinctively. See [Post-Session Review](/learn/execution/post-session-review/) and [Trade Journaling](/learn/execution/trade-journaling/) for what that looks like in practice.

---

## A worked example: a simple weekday routine

A routine doesn't need to be elaborate to hold together. A version built around a London-session day trader:

> **06:45** Check the economic calendar and any overnight news for the instruments traded
> **06:55** Mark key levels: previous day's high/low, week's opening level
> **07:00** Confirm today's risk limit and run the pre-session checklist
> **08:00–11:30** Trading window, no new entries after 11:00
> **11:30** Log every trade taken, including the reason for entry at the time
> **Weekly, Friday evening** Reread the week's log, check win rate, average R, and adherence to the plan

Nothing on that list is complicated on its own. The value comes from doing the same version of it every session, not from any single step being clever.

---

## The routine changes as the trader does

A routine built in the first months of trading a strategy won't necessarily fit a year later, and treating it as fixed forever is its own kind of mistake. A step that earned its place after a specific early error, an instrument mix-up, a missed calendar check, can eventually become automatic enough that it's checked without conscious thought, at which point it stops needing to be a deliberate line item. New steps get added the same way the original ones did, in response to a real gap the current routine doesn't cover, instead of by trying to design a perfect routine once and never touching it again.

---

## Key takeaways

- A pre-session routine, checking the calendar, marking levels, reviewing risk limits, closes off a lot of avoidable drift before it starts
- Fixed session hours remove the excuse to keep trading past the point judgement has already slipped
- A proper post-session review checks whether each trade matched the plan, not just whether the day was profitable
- A simple, specific routine, timed and written down, holds together better than a vague intention to "be organised"
- A routine isn't fixed forever, it evolves as old steps become automatic and new gaps get found
