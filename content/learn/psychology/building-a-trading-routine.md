A strategy decides what to trade, a routine decides how the session around it runs, and a repeatable routine matters as much as the strategy itself, because most of the mistakes covered elsewhere in this section happen in the gaps a routine is supposed to fill.

---

## Before the session

Preparation before the market opens does two things at once: it puts the day's real catalysts, scheduled data, key levels, anything that changes the plan, in view before they can surprise a live position, and it gives the trader a calm moment to commit to the day's rules before pressure has a chance to bend them. A quick routine, checking the calendar, marking the levels that matter, reviewing the plan's risk limits for the day, takes a few minutes and closes off a lot of the drift that shows up later.

---

## During the session

A session with a defined start and end time behaves differently to one that just continues until the trader happens to stop, because an open-ended session removes the one honest signal that it might be time to walk away. Fixed hours don't guarantee good decisions on their own, but they remove the excuse to keep going past the point where focus and judgement have already started to slip, which several other topics in this section cover from different angles.

---

## After the session

The review that happens after the session closes matters more than most traders give it credit for, and it's not the same thing as checking the day's P&L. A proper review looks at whether each trade matched the plan's criteria regardless of whether it won or lost, because a losing trade taken correctly and a winning trade taken outside the rules deserve the opposite reaction to the one most traders give them instinctively.

---

## Key takeaways

- A pre-session routine, checking the calendar, marking levels, reviewing risk limits, closes off a lot of avoidable drift before it starts
- Fixed session hours remove the excuse to keep trading past the point judgement has already slipped
- A proper post-session review checks whether each trade matched the plan, not just whether the day was profitable
- The routine around a strategy matters as much as the strategy, because most of the mistakes this section covers happen in the gaps a routine fills
