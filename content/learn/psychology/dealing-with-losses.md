Every strategy loses some of the time, that's not a flaw to fix, it's a cost built into the maths from the start, which makes how a trader handles the losses that do come at least as important as how they handle the wins.

---

## Every strategy loses, that's priced in

A strategy with a genuine edge can still have a win rate under 50%, wins bigger than losses, and still come out ahead over enough trades, which means losing individual trades is completely normal and expected, not a sign anything went wrong. Treating every loss as a problem to solve misunderstands what the strategy's own statistics already assume will happen.

*Example: a strategy with a 40% win rate and an average win of 2.5R against an average loss of 1R is a real, profitable edge, and it still means six or seven losses out of every ten trades. A trader expecting to win most of the time will misread perfectly normal results as a broken strategy.*

---

## The two different reasons a trade loses

Not all losses carry the same information, and mixing them up is where most of the damage happens. A trade taken per plan, correct setup, right size, stop placed where the strategy says it goes, that simply doesn't work out is a normal statistical loss, and it says nothing about the trader or the strategy beyond confirming that no edge wins every time. A trade that loses because the setup wasn't really there, the size was wrong, or the stop got moved is a different kind of loss, one worth a proper review, because it points at an error, not ordinary variance.

---

## A losing streak isn't automatically a warning sign

Several losses in a row feels like evidence something has gone wrong, but a genuine edge with a sub-50% win rate will produce real losing streaks on a fairly regular basis, that's what the win rate means in practice, not a hypothetical. The better question isn't "why is this happening," it's the same one from the section above, applied trade by trade across the streak: was each individual loss taken correctly per plan, or did the streak itself start eroding discipline partway through. A streak of five correctly-taken losses is normal variance. A streak where the third loss triggered oversized sizing on the fourth is a different, more serious problem wearing the same statistical costume.

---

## Don't let this loss decide the next trade

The real risk after a loss isn't the loss itself, it's letting it change the size, entry criteria, or confidence behind the next trade for reasons that have nothing to do with that next trade's own setup. A properly planned loss should change nothing about the next decision; a loss that reveals a genuine mistake should change the process, through deliberate review, not through a smaller or more hesitant version of the very next trade taken under the same emotional cloud.

---

## Key takeaways

- Losing is a normal, expected part of any strategy with a real edge, not evidence something went wrong
- A loss taken per plan is a statistical loss and needs no changes; a loss caused by a real mistake needs a proper review
- A losing streak is only a warning sign if discipline slipped partway through it, not simply because several losses happened in a row
- Don't let a loss change the size or confidence behind the next unrelated trade
- Review process changes deliberately, away from the emotional pull of the trade that just closed
