Fear, greed, and FOMO aren't vague personality flaws that only affect undisciplined traders, they're three emotional states that show up in almost every account at some point, and each one pushes toward a specific, recognisable mistake, not a vague one.

---

## Fear

Fear shows up in two opposite-looking ways that come from the same place. It stops a trader taking a valid setup because the last two trades lost and the chart in front of them now feels untrustworthy, even though nothing about the setup itself has changed. And it closes a winning trade early, well before the planned target, because being up feels fragile and locking in something small feels safer than risking it turning into nothing. Both are the same instinct: treat the current position as more dangerous than the plan says it is, and act to reduce that danger right now instead of at the level the plan already chose.

---

## Greed

Greed is the opposite pull: a winning trade that's hit its target starts to feel like it should be given more room, because the market's clearly moving and closing now leaves obvious money on the table. It also shows up before a trade is even open, as oversizing a setup that looks unusually good, on the reasoning that a strong signal deserves a bigger bet than the plan's normal risk. Both versions treat a good outcome as evidence the rules can be stretched for it, which is backwards: a plan that only gets followed on the losing trades and abandoned on the winning ones isn't really a plan.

---

## FOMO

Fear of missing out is the one most traders recognise fastest in themselves, and it's usually the most expensive. It's the urge to enter a move that's already well underway, after the original setup that would have justified the trade is long gone, purely because the chart is moving and not being in it feels like losing. An entry taken this way has no real stop logic behind it, because there was no plan behind the entry in the first place, just the discomfort of watching a move happen without you.

---

## Key takeaways

- Fear shows up as skipping valid setups after recent losses and closing winners early out of a need for safety
- Greed shows up as letting winners run past a planned target or oversizing a trade that "looks too good to pass up"
- FOMO is entering a move that's already happened, with no real setup or stop logic behind the entry
- All three share the same underlying move: reacting to how a trade currently feels instead of what the plan already decided
