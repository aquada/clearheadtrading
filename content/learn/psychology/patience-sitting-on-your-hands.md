Doing nothing looks like the absence of a decision, but in trading it's a position in its own right, and it's very often the correct one. Patience shows up in two different places in a trade's life, and most traders are only good at one of them.

---

## Waiting for the setup, not just a setup

The first kind of patience is refusing to trade until the market offers the specific conditions a strategy is built around, instead of something that resembles them closely enough to feel tempting. A strategy built on a clean break of a key level doesn't get to skip that requirement because three hours have passed with nothing happening and a near-miss just showed up instead. The setup that's 80% there isn't a slightly worse version of the real thing, it's a different trade with a different, usually worse, expectancy behind it.

*Example: a trader waiting for a confirmed breakout watches price approach a level three separate times over an hour without closing above it. On the fourth approach, tired of waiting, they enter early on the wick alone. Price rejects the level for a fourth time. The three earlier approaches weren't bad luck to be annoyed about, they were the market correctly failing to meet the entry criteria.*

---

## Patience once you're in

The second kind is harder for most traders to build: staying in a trade that's still following the plan, without adjusting it every time the price wobbles. A position that's up but hasn't hit target yet doesn't need managing every five minutes, and a position that's dipped into a drawdown the original stop already accounts for doesn't need rescuing. The plan already decided what happens at the stop and what happens at the target; checking in on it constantly mostly just creates opportunities to override that decision early.

---

## Patience isn't the same as hesitation

It helps to separate genuine patience from a look-alike that costs just as much: hesitating on a setup that has already met every one of the plan's criteria. Patience is refusing to act until the criteria are met. Hesitation is failing to act once they already have been, usually out of a lingering doubt that has nothing to do with the setup itself. A trader who waits correctly for three hours and then freezes for thirty extra seconds once the real signal finally appears has swapped one problem for another, not solved it.

---

## Boredom is a real risk, not something to push through

Waiting for a real setup can mean long stretches with nothing worth trading, and that idle time is when patience is most likely to fail, not because the market got harder to read, but because sitting still starts to feel like wasted time. A trader who's bored is a trader looking for a reason to act, and markets are very good at offering something that looks like a reason if you're actively searching for one. Recognising boredom for what it is, a state that makes a mediocre setup look better than it is, is most of what separates genuine patience from just not having found anything to do yet.

---

## Key takeaways

- Waiting for the exact setup your strategy is built around, not a close approximation of it, is the first kind of patience
- Staying in a valid trade without managing it every few minutes is the second, harder kind
- Patience is waiting until the criteria are met; hesitation is freezing once they already have been, and the two aren't the same problem
- Boredom during a quiet market is a real risk factor, it makes weaker setups look more tempting than they are
- Doing nothing is a legitimate position, not a gap where a decision should have been made
