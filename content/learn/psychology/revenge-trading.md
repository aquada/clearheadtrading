Revenge trading is what happens when a loss stops being information and starts being a debt that needs settling immediately, and it's one of the most reliable ways to turn a single bad trade into three.

---

## How it starts

The pattern begins with a loss that feels unfair, a stop hit just before the market reversed, a setup that looked textbook and failed anyway, and instead of accepting it as one of the outcomes the strategy's own statistics already price in, it gets reframed as an injustice that needs correcting. The next trade isn't chosen because a real setup appeared, it's chosen because a loss needs undoing, and those are two completely different reasons to enter a position.

---

## The warning signs before the trade even happens

Revenge trading rarely starts with a calm, considered decision, it has a recognisable build-up. Refreshing the chart more often than usual right after a loss, feeling a pull toward instruments outside the normal plan, or catching the thought "just get back to breakeven" forming before any real setup has appeared, are all signs the next trade is about to be chosen for the wrong reason. Recognising the build-up before the entry is placed is the only point where it's still easy to stop.

---

## Why it compounds instead of fixing anything

A trade entered to win back a loss almost always breaks at least one normal rule: it's sized bigger than usual, because a smaller position wouldn't feel like it makes up for what just happened, or it's entered without the same setup criteria, because waiting for a proper one feels intolerable in that state of mind. Bigger size on a lower-quality entry is close to the worst combination available, and when that trade loses too, which it often does, the same urgency is now attached to a bigger number.

*Example: a trader risking 1% per trade loses a normal, plan-following trade. Instead of stopping there, they take a revenge trade sized at 2% to "get it back faster." That one loses too. Now down 3% instead of 1%, with two undisciplined entries added to what started as one clean, expected loss.*

---

## The fix is a rule, not willpower

Knowing about revenge trading rarely stops it happening in the moment, because the moment is precisely when judgement is least reliable. What works is a mechanical rule decided in advance, a fixed pause after any loss before a new trade is allowed, or a hard stop for the day once a certain loss total is hit, so the decision doesn't depend on willpower holding up at the exact point it's weakest. A short, enforced pause, stepping away from the screen for even ten minutes, is often enough on its own, not because ten minutes changes the market, but because it removes the trade from the exact window where the urge is strongest.

---

## Key takeaways

- Revenge trading starts when a loss gets treated as an injustice to correct instead of information the strategy already expects
- Warning signs, refreshing the chart obsessively, drifting toward unusual instruments, the thought "just get back to breakeven" appear before the trade itself does
- The revenge trade is usually oversized and taken without the normal setup criteria, a worse entry with more size behind it
- It compounds the damage because it adds a second, lower-quality loss on top of the first
- A mechanical rule decided in advance, a cooling-off period or a daily loss limit, works better than relying on willpower in the moment
